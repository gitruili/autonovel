**10. THE FIRST MOURNER**

*HOOK:* The first human to die didn't die—they chose to let go, and their decision created the boundary between life and death that everyone else has been trapped within ever since. Someone wants to undo it.

*WORLD:* The Threshold—the edge of existence, a place that isn't quite anywhere. Time moves differently; memories drift like sediment. The landscape is made of accumulated deaths: a forest of regret, a river of last words, a mountain of all the things left unsaid.

*MAGIC/COST:* Memory-anchor—the ability to hold a death in your mind without being consumed by it. Each death you witness takes up space in you; carry too many and you become a graveyard. The first mourner was the one who learned to witness without becoming.

*TENSION:* Personal: She has been carrying her mother's death for years, holding it carefully, refusing to let it go. She has become the Threshold's keeper. But her mother wants to move on, and keeping her means keeping her trapped. Cosmic: Someone is trying to undo the first death—unmake the boundary between existence and absence. If they succeed, nothing will ever end. Nothing will ever begin.

*THEME:* Is the boundary between life and death a prison, or is letting go a kind of mercy?

*WHY IT'S NOT GENERIC:* A quiet, melancholy meditation on mortality that makes death itself the protagonist's antagonist. No chosen ones, no dark lords—just the universal experience of loss, given a body.
